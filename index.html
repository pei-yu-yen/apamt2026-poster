Are They Really Harmless?

Essential Oil and Aroma Product Exposures Reported to Taiwan Poison Control Center, 2022–2025

01   STUDY OVERVIEW

Essential oils and aroma products are widely used in households and are often perceived as natural and safe. However, accidental exposures continue to be reported, particularly among young children.

This retrospective study reviewed 142 reported exposures to essential oils and aroma products received by Taiwan Poison Control Center between 2022 and 2025. Although most cases had no symptoms reported, one severe and one fatal outcome were recorded.

For detailed product categories, age distributions, exposure routes, symptoms and severity classifications, please refer to the full poster (PDF).

02   BEYOND THE POSTER

Product Identification: A Challenge in Exposure Assessment

Essential oil and aroma products vary in their intended uses and available ingredient information. Classification in this study relied on caller-reported product names, intended uses, and available ingredient details. Incomplete identification may limit the precision of exposure assessment and interpretation.

Understanding Age-Related Exposure Patterns

Exposures were predominantly reported among young children, particularly for diffuser products. This study did not directly investigate product accessibility, packaging, storage practices, or caregiver supervision. These are potential topics for future research, not established explanations for the observed patterns.

Interpreting Clinical Outcomes

Symptom reporting and clinical outcome severity describe related but distinct aspects of case assessment. The presence or absence of reported symptoms does not, by itself, fully characterize severity. Incomplete outcome assessments should also be considered when interpreting severity distributions.

What Can Poison Center Data Tell Us?

Poison center records can identify reported exposure patterns and potential public health concerns. However, voluntary reporting and incomplete product or follow-up information limit interpretation. These descriptive data should not be treated as population-based incidence estimates.

03   POINTS FOR DISCUSSION

Beyond the descriptive findings, this study raises questions that may warrant further investigation.

How might product formulation, packaging, and accessibility influence exposure patterns among young children?

The observed age distribution highlights possible product-related and environmental factors, but further research would be needed to determine their contributions.

What additional data would improve the assessment of essential oil and aroma product exposures in poison center surveillance?

More complete information about ingredients, exposure circumstances, clinical course, and outcomes may strengthen future analyses.
